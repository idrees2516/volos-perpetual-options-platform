   171→   171→2. The key strategic insight: don't compete with Derive on Derive's strongest feature
   172→   172→Derive has already done much of the very difficult infrastructure work.
   173→   173→
   174→   174→It offers portfolio margin, cross-asset collateral and multiple subaccounts, with its risk engine calculating maximum portfolio loss under spot and volatility shocks and adding contingencies for basis, oracles and other risks. Derive says its system recognizes offsets between multi-leg structures instead of margining every leg independently. (insights.derive.xyz)
   175→   175→
   176→   176→Paradex is building in essentially the same professional direction. Its portfolio-margin system currently stress-tests portfolios across 24 simultaneous spot/IV scenarios, although portfolio margin remains beta/whitelisted. (docs.paradex.trade)
   177→   177→
   178→   178→And Paradigm has already built sophisticated institutional multi-leg liquidity discovery. Paradigm's RFQ model supports private auctions, multiple makers and block-trade settlement in which all legs clear atomically or the entire trade is rejected. (Paradigm Documentation)
   179→   179→
   180→   180→Therefore I would not spend the first two years recreating:
   181→   181→
   182→   182→matching engine + appchain + margin engine + market-maker API + new token incentives
   183→   183→
   184→   184→just to arrive where these projects already are.
   185→   185→
   186→   186→Instead:
   187→   187→
   188→   188→Use existing venues as liquidity backends and build the product layer they have not yet fully solved.

   195→   195→UX, demand, collateral efficiency, education, two-sided liquidity and professional market-making infrastructure.

   402→   402→8. Routing should occur at the strategy level
   403→   403→This is a big distinction from a conventional aggregator.
   404→   404→
   405→   405→Suppose the user wants:
   406→   406→
   407→   407→100 BTC 100k/120k call spreads.
   408→   408→
   409→   409→A naïve router says:
   410→   410→
   411→   411→Venue A has the cheapest 100k calls.
   412→   412→Venue B has the best bid for 120k calls.
   413→   413→
   414→   414→That can be disastrous.
   415→   415→
   416→   416→You buy the calls on A.
   417→   417→
   418→   418→BTC jumps.
   419→   419→
   420→   420→Before you sell the calls on B, the market moves.
   421→   421→
   422→   422→You've acquired enormous unintended delta and vega exposure.
   423→   423→
   424→   424→So the objective function should be:
   425→   425→
   426→   426→best executable price for the entire payoff.
   427→   427→
   428→   428→Paradigm already demonstrates the importance of this concept. Its block trades are sent to settlement venues atomically; if one leg cannot clear, the block is rejected. Paradex's own RFQ API now supports up to 25 legs, including strategy legs and fixed-price hedge legs. (Paradigm Documentation)
   429→   429→
   430→   430→This tells us that strategy-native trading is technically quite realistic.

   432→   432→9. How the router should decide where to send an order
   433→   433→Execution could roughly follow:
   434→   434→
   435→   435→Trade   Preferred execution
   436→   436→Small liquid single option      CLOB
   437→   437→Standard spread Combo/strategy book
   438→   438→Large spread    Multi-dealer RFQ
   439→   439→Very large institutional position       Private RFQ
   440→   440→Long-tail token AMM/perpetual primitive
   441→   441→Highly customized strategy      Solver / RFQ
   442→   442→Automated roll  Calendar-combo/RFQ
   443→   443→Delta-neutral structure Options + fixed hedge leg
   444→   444→The cost function shouldn't simply be:
   445→   445→
   446→   446→lowest premium.
   447→   447→
   448→   448→It should approximately be:
   449→   449→
   450→   450→**option price
   451→   451→
   452→   452→venue fees
   453→   453→gas
   454→   454→bid/ask slippage
   455→   455→expected hedge slippage
   456→   456→collateral opportunity cost
   457→   457→borrowing/funding
   458→   458→settlement risk
   459→   459→bridge/transfer cost.**
   460→   460→That is much closer to true execution quality.

   463→   463→The architecture of CoW Protocol and intent systems provides an interesting model.
   464→   464→
   465→   465→CoW uses combinatorial batch auctions and competing solvers to source liquidity. More generally, ERC-7683 now describes a solver-facing framework in which a user specifies an outcome and specialized fillers determine how to fulfill it. (docs.cow.fi)
   466→   466→
   467→   467→Imagine applying that model to an option strategy.
   468→   468→
   469→   469→The user signs:
   470→   470→
   471→   471→Deliver +100 BTC 100k calls
   472→   472→and −100 BTC 120k calls
   473→   473→expiring October 30
   474→   474→net debit ≤ $820,000
   475→   475→before 14:
   476→   476→before 14:01:30 UTC.
   477→   477→
   478→   478→Several solvers compete.
   479→   479→
   480→   480→Solver A may execute entirely on Derive.
   481→   481→
   482→   482→Solver B may warehouse the option risk and hedge through Deribit.
   483→   483→
   484→   484→Solver C may quote using Paradex and its own inventory.
   485→   485→
   486→   486→Solver D may cross against another client intent.
   487→   487→
   488→   488→The user doesn't care.
   489→   489→
   490→   490→They receive the desired economic position at the best eligible firm price.
   491→   491→
   492→   492→This is potentially much bigger than a simple DEX aggregator.

   494→   494→11. But cross-venue atomicity is the hardest problem
   495→   495→This needs to be stated explicitly because many “aggregator” ideas hide it.
   496→   496→
   497→   497→A trade cannot magically be atomic across:
   498→   498→
   499→   499→Derive + Paradex + Deribit + a CEX + an AMM
   500→   500→
   501→   501→unless some intermediary takes settlement risk.
   502→   502→
   503→   503→Therefore cross-venue strategy execution ultimately requires one of three models.
   504→   504→
   505→   505→Model   How it works    Trade-off
   506→   506→Same-venue package      Entire strategy stays on one venue      Safest, less price competition
   507→   507→Sequential routing      Router executes legs in sequence        Leg risk
   508→   508→Principal solver        Solver gives user firm package price and hedges wherever it wants       Solver needs capital and risk infrastructure
   509→   509→The third model is the most powerful.
   510→   510→
   511→   511→It is also where the moat develops.
   512→   512→
   513→   513→The solver is effectively saying:
   514→   514→
   515→   515→“I guarantee you the strategy at this price. Whatever happens while I hedge it is my problem.”
   516→   516→
   517→   517→Now sophisticated market makers become part of the product's infrastructure.

   519→   519→12. Feature #3 — atomic strategy markets
   520→   520→This should be treated as a first-class primitive.
   521→   521→
   522→   522→An iron condor isn't four unrelated options.
   523→   523→
   524→   524→It's one economic position.
   525→   525→
   526→   526→A collar isn't three positions.
   527→   527→
   528→   528→It's one risk-management transaction.
   529→   529→
   530→   530→Paradigm's current public orderbook concept already supports globally available strategies containing multiple instruments and quantity ratios, while its RFQ workflow supports private one- or multi-leg structures. (Paradigm Documentation)
   531→   531→
   532→   532→Your internal object therefore shouldn't be:
   533→   533→
   534→   534→Order
   535→   535→
   536→   536→It should be:
   537→   537→
   538→   538→StrategyOrder.
   539→   539→
   540→   540→Conceptually:
   541→   541→
   542→   542→Parameter       Example
   543→   543→Legs    Four
   544→   544→Ratios  +1 / −1 / −1 / +1
   545→   545→Net direction   Buy
   546→   546→Quantity        100
   547→   547→Limit   $X debit
   548→   548→FOK     True
   549→   549→Allowed venues  Derive, Paradex
   550→   550→Hedge leg       Optional BTC perp
   551→   551→Anonymous       Yes
   552→   552→Quote validity  5 sec
   553→   553→Maximum slippage        5 bps equivalent
   554→   554→Margin pre-check        Passed
   555→   555→Every quote returned by a market maker would be for the net package price.
   556→   556→
   557→   557→That makes option strategies behave more like perpetual-futures orders.

   767→   767→18. Feature #5 — persistent positions / Auto-Roll
   768→   768→I think this could actually become more important than “true” perpetual options.
   769→   769→
   770→   770→Instead of inventing a new derivative, give a dated option a perpetual user experience.
   771→   771→
   772→   772→The user asks:
   773→   773→
   774→   774→“Always keep BTC protected 20% below spot.”
   775→   775→

   777→   777→
   778→   778→Underneath it might hold:
   779→   779→
   780→   780→today:
   781→   781→
   782→   782→30D 80%-moneyness BTC put
   783→   783→
   784→   784→and when 7 DTE remains:
   785→   785→
   786→   786→sell it and buy:
   787→   787→
   788→   788→new 30D 80%-moneyness put.
   789→   789→
   790→   790→Traditional finance already uses systematic rolling extensively; Cboe maintains rule-based option indices with explicit roll methodologies, and institutional broker tools already automate/assist option rolls.
   791→   791→
   792→   792→The opportunity is to make this native to crypto portfolios.
   793→   793→
   794→   794→19. There should be several roll policies
   795→   795→A persistent option position needs a policy definition.
   796→   796→
   797→   797→Policy  Meaning
   798→   798→Constant maturity       Always maintain ~30 DTE
   799→   799→Constant delta  Always maintain ~25Δ put
   800→   800→Constant moneyness      Always maintain 20% OTM
   801→   801→Constant premium        Spend $500/month
   802→   802→Constant portfolio protection   Target max portfolio drawdown
   803→   803→Constant gamma  Maintain target convexity
   804→   804→Event-aware     Don't roll through specified event
   805→   805→Carry-aware     Choose dated/perpetual based on cost
   806→   806→This is where options become dramatically more useful for wealth/treasury management.
   807→   807→
   808→   808→20. Rolling must itself be atomic
   809→   809→You don't want:
   810→   810→
   811→   811→sell old put → market crashes → cannot buy new put.
   812→   812→
   813→   813→So a roll should be represented as a calendar spread package:
   814→   814→
   815→   815→sell old protection
   816→   816→
   817→   817→buy new protection
   818→   818→at one net price.
   819→   819→
   820→   820→Again, strategy execution solves the problem.
   821→   821→
   822→   822→21. Auto-roll needs strict execution rules
   823→   823→Automation should be allowed to skip a roll if conditions are unacceptable.
   824→   824→
   825→   825→Example policy:
   826→   826→
   827→   827→Guardrail       Rule
   828→   828→Max spread      3% premium
   829→   829→Max roll debit  $2,000
   830→   830→Minimum maker quotes    3
   831→   831→Minimum quote size      100% required
   832→   832→Oracle confidence       High
   833→   833→Maximum IV percentile   95th
   834→   834→Emergency condition     Never go completely unhedged
   835→   835→If liquidity is bad, don't blindly execute because a cron job fired.
   836→   836→
   837→   837→This is one reason an options automation system needs a risk engine, not merely a scheduler.

  1242→  1242→32. Feature #8 — unified collateral and volatility prime brokerage
  1243→  1243→This is potentially the largest institutional opportunity.
  1244→  1244→
  1245→  1245→It is also the one most often described too casually.
  1246→  1246→
  1247→  1247→You cannot simply claim:
  1248→  1248→

  1250→  1250→
  1251→  1251→Each venue independently controls its collateral.
  1252→  1252→
  1253→  1253→If one venue liquidates you, the fact that another account has excess collateral doesn't necessarily save you.
  1254→  1254→

  1256→  1256→

  1259→  1259→2       Automated collateral allocator  Medium

  1287→  1287→liquidation buffer

  1375→  1375→36. Cross-venue portfolio margin is potentially the ultimate moat
  1376→  1376→Suppose the user's true portfolio is:

  1386→  1386→

  1388→  1388→
  1389→  1389→But you need the ability to enforce claims across every venue before you can safely give the savings.
  1390→  1390→

  1392→  1392→
  1393→  1393→So I would not build this first.

  1397→  1397→37. Feature #9 — long-tail options
